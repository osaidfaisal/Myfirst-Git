Study smarter with Claude:
a beginner's guide on the free plan

No tech background needed and no subscription. Follow the steps below in order and you can save 5 or more hours every week on readings, assignments, emails and planning.

✓ Works on the free plan✓ Phone or laptop✓ English, Urdu or Roman Urdu

Part 1: Set up Claude in 5 minutes

  1. Open Claude. On a laptop, go to claude.ai. On a phone, install the free Claude app (made by Anthropic) from the App Store or Google Play.
  2. Sign up for free. Tap "Continue with Google" or enter your email. Skip any paid-plan screen, because the free plan is all you need for this guide.
  3. Find the message box. It sits at the bottom of the screen. Type there just as you would in WhatsApp, then press Enter or tap the arrow.
  4. Find the "+" or 📎 button. It lets you attach a PDF, a screenshot or a photo of your notes, and Claude can read all of these.
  5. Phone tip: tap the 🎤 microphone on your keyboard and just talk to type your question. This is great when you're tired or walking to class.
  6. Say hi to test it. Paste this message as your first one:
    I'm a first-year student at UC Davis from Pakistan, majoring in Computer Science. I'm new to AI tools. In 5 short bullet points, tell me the most useful ways you can help me this quarter.

💡 The one rule to remember

Treat Claude like a smart senior student or a TA sitting next to you, not like Google. Talk to it in full sentences, give it background and ask follow-up questions. If an answer isn't right, reply "make it simpler" or "shorter" or "give an example". You don't need to start over.

🗣️ Urdu is fine too

Claude understands Urdu and Roman Urdu. Try: "Mujhe recursion bilkul samajh nahi aaya, Urdu mein simple example ke saath samjhao." You can also ask it to explain something in Urdu first and then in English.

Part 2

The CHAI formula ☕ for great answers every time

Most beginners type 4 words and get a generic answer. To get an answer that fits you, include these 4 things in your message:

C
Context
Who are you? Which class, what level?
H
Help needed
What exactly do you want done?
A
Add details
Deadline, word count, your notes, rubric
I
Ideal format
Bullets? Table? Quiz? 5 lines max?
Weak prompt
explain supply and demand

This gets a textbook-style answer that is long, generic and hard to revise from.

CHAI prompt
C: I'm a first-year in ECN 1A at UC Davis. H: Explain supply and demand so I can answer midterm questions. A: My midterm is in 4 days. Use examples about chai, rent in Davis or gas prices. I: 5 bullet points, then 3 practice questions with answers hidden at the bottom.

Part 3: The magic follow-ups

The first answer is only a draft. These short replies make it better:

Type this……when
Explain like I'm 12.The answer is too technical
Give me a real-life example.You understand the words but not the idea
Quiz me one question at a time.You want to check whether you've actually learned it
What did I get wrong, and why?After you answer its quiz questions
Make it shorter. Max 5 lines.The answer is too long
Ask me 3 questions first before you answer.You aren't sure what to include and want Claude to interview you
Are you sure? Double-check that.Anything involving facts, numbers, dates or citations

⚠️ Claude can be wrong

AI sometimes states wrong facts confidently, and it can even make up sources. Always verify deadlines, formulas, citations, visa rules and anything important against your syllabus, Canvas, your professor or official UC Davis offices.

Part 4

12 copy-paste prompts that save hours

Copy a prompt, replace the yellow parts with your own details, and send it.

01Understand a confusing lecture

📎 [attach slides or a photo of notes] I didn't understand this lecture on topic. Explain the 3 most important ideas simply, with one example each. Then tell me what's most likely to be on the exam.

02Turn notes into a quiz

📎 [attach notes] Make 10 practice questions from these notes: 5 multiple-choice and 5 short-answer. Ask me one at a time and wait for my answer before giving feedback.

03Read a long PDF in 10 minutes

📎 [attach reading] Summarize this reading for class: the main argument, 3 key points with page numbers, and 2 questions I could raise in discussion section.

04Plan the 10-week quarter

📎 [attach syllabi] Here are my syllabi. List every exam and deadline by week (1–10 plus finals). Warn me about weeks where several things are due at once.

05Get essay feedback (not a ghostwriter)

📎 [attach your draft + rubric] Don't rewrite this. Act as a strict grader using this rubric. Give me a score, my 3 biggest weaknesses and questions that help me fix them myself.

06Email a professor

Help me write a short, polite email to Professor name. I missed a quiz because I was sick and want to ask about options. Respectful American academic tone, not overly formal, under 120 words.

07Prepare for office hours

I'm going to office hours for class. I'm stuck on problem. Help me write 3 clear, specific questions so I don't waste the professor's time.

08Understand code errors

I'm learning Python. Here is my code and the error message. Don't just fix it. Explain what the error means, give me a hint, and let me try again. [paste code + error]

09Improve a resume line

Rewrite this resume bullet for a US internship application. Use a strong action verb and add a number if possible. Give 3 options: "I helped in my uncle's shop in Lahore"

10Practice an interview

Act as an interviewer for a software intern role. Ask me one behavioral question at a time. After each of my answers, score it out of 10 and show me how to improve it using the STAR method.

11Plan your week around everything

Make a weekly schedule table for me. Classes: list. Campus job: hours. Include the 5 daily prayers, Jumu'ah on Friday, meals, the gym and 2 study blocks a day. I study best in the morning.

12Explain American "adulting"

I'm a new international student from Pakistan. Explain what a credit score is / how a lease works / how US tipping works in simple words, with mistakes newcomers usually make.

Part 5

Get the most out of the free plan

The free plan has a message limit that resets every few hours. These habits help you stay under it:

1. Put everything in one message

Send one detailed CHAI prompt rather than 6 tiny messages, which use up your limit quickly.

2. Start a new chat for each new topic

Long chats use more of your limit. When you switch subjects, click "New chat".

3. Upload only the pages you need

Attach chapter 4, not the full 300-page textbook. Screenshots of the key pages work too.

4. Copy great answers somewhere safe

Paste useful answers into Google Docs or Notes so you don't have to ask again.

5. Work during off-peak hours

If you hit the limit, the app tells you when it resets. Plan your heavy study with Claude around that time.

6. Ask for a summary before moving on

Before you leave a long chat, type: "Summarize everything we figured out in 10 bullets." Paste that into a new chat to continue.

🛑 Academic integrity: please read this

  • Every class has its own AI policy. Check the syllabus, or ask the professor: "Is using AI for brainstorming / feedback / studying allowed?"
  • Submitting AI-written work as your own when it isn't allowed can be treated as academic misconduct. For international students, that can put more than your grade at risk.
  • The safe and smart way: use Claude to explain, quiz, plan and give feedback. You do the actual writing and problem-solving yourself.
  • Never paste other people's private information, exam questions you were told not to share, or passwords into any AI tool.
